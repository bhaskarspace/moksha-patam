# Landscape: what else is out there

Scanned 18 Oct 2026. Companion to `REDESIGN.md`. The short version: the board is
crowded, the philosophy is not. Nobody is teaching the darśanas as an argument.

---

## 1. Same board, different purpose

**The Leela ecosystem** is the closest sibling and it is well established.

Harish Johari's *The Yoga of Snakes and Arrows: The Leela of Self-Knowledge* (Inner
Traditions) put a 72-square board into print as a self-inquiry tool. Note the square
count: 72 is the medieval Hindu board documented in `REFERENCE.md §4`, so this is the
historical board, not an invention. Snakes and **arrows** rather than ladders.

Digital versions exist and are active:

- **leela.guide** — free browser version, 72 squares, ten snakes and ten arrows. You
  enter the board only on a six, landing at Birth, then advance to square 6
  (Delusion). You state a question before playing and talk each square through with
  one of five LLM "guides" (Ananta, Daya, Kavya, Tattva, Veer). Free for ten AI
  replies, then an account. Framed as reflection, explicitly not prediction.
- **Leela: The Game of Knowledge** (iOS), **LeelaRoom**, a **Leela Quest** Chrome
  extension, and various retreat and workshop uses.

**gyanchaupar.co.uk** sells a physical board and poster, "the modern spiritual game of
Snakes & Ladders". Start in a Mandala of Unity, enter on a 3 or 6, chakra squares let a
6 jump you to the next chakra, finish at Enlightenment on an exact roll. Deliberately
has no fixed rules, only guidelines, and an Infinite Play Mode that continues after
someone finishes. Reflection is the whole activity.

**Rebirth: The Tibetan Game of Liberation** (*sa lam*) is the Buddhist cousin,
published by Shang Shung Publications and run as workshop events.

**Mass-market apps.** Plenty of Indian-skinned Snakes and Ladders on Play Store, for
example Gameroost's *Snake & Ladder – Mokshapatam* out of Hyderabad. These are the
standard game with the old name on it and no content.

**Read:** the oracle and self-reflection register is thoroughly taken, by people who
got there first and do it well. Do not compete there. The Leela products do not name a
single philosophical school; they use the board as a mirror. That is a different
product from a game that teaches you what Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā disagreed about.

## 2. Closest competitor in concept

**Mumukshu** (2025), by Sahil Shirke, Mumbai. An Indian life-simulation board game and
the only thing found that overlaps the redesign's actual idea.

- Players play as themselves, competing to live an ideal life.
- Resource management and decisions rather than luck.
- Mechanics: wealth plus *kṛpā* (divine grace) points earned through righteous action,
  spent to activate a *siddhi*; *māyā* as a derailing force; balancing five areas
  including family, society and dharma; professions drawn from the Upavedas.
- Uses the puruṣārthas, gṛhastha dharma, māyā and karma.
- Board maps Akhaṇḍ Bhārat. Artwork commissioned across 21 Indian art styles.
- Physical board first; digital and VR stated as later.

**Read:** the overlap is real on puruṣārthas, karma and grace, and the kṛpā mechanic is
close to the Dvaita law in the plan. The differences are large enough to matter.
Mumukshu is physical-first, competitive, values-and-civilisation framed, and oriented
toward an ideal life rather than toward the disagreements between schools. A free
browser game in English and Hindi, built around the ṣaḍ-darśana as six incompatible
causal laws, is not the same product. Worth watching when its digital version lands.

## 3. Games that actually teach philosophy

**Socrates Jones: Pro Philosopher** is the most useful precedent in this whole scan,
and it is Western. Built by students and faculty at Carnegie Mellon, free on Kongregate
in 2013, rebuilt for Steam in 2023, over 300,000 plays by 2014.

The mechanic is argument. You examine each sentence an opponent says and can ask for
clarification, ask how it is relevant, or demand supporting evidence. Claims must come
from a "common ground" both parties accept. You win a round by countering a claim with
something you gathered earlier. A credibility meter drains on mistakes. Difficulty
rises along a chronological curve through Euthyphro, Protagoras, Hobbes, Mill and Kant.
The ad hominem option ("your face is ugly") always fails, which teaches by refusing to
work. The designers accepted valid arguments they had not anticipated.

Criticism worth noting: *The Gameological Society* called it a novel introduction that
never goes beyond that, and reviewers found it wordy.

**Others:** *Philosophy Experiments* (web, thought-experiment quizzes), *Quandary*
(ethics for children), and philosophy-as-theme in larger games including *The Talos
Principle* (consciousness and personhood), *Disco Elysium* (ideology as a character
system), and *Everything* (narrated with Alan Watts).

## 4. The itch.io layer

A cluster of small games named *Samsara*, *Karma*, *Chakra*, *Wheel of Samsara*,
*Crossing Samsara*, plus *Maximum Recursion Depth*, a self-described "karmapunk" RPG.
Mostly jam-scale work using the vocabulary for atmosphere. No sustained treatment of
the schools. Not competition, but worth a look for art direction.

## 5. The gap

Put the four groups side by side and the hole is clean.

| | Board | Teaches a school | Free & web | Bilingual | Sourced |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leela ecosystem | yes | no | partly | some | no |
| Mumukshu | yes | partly | not yet | — | no |
| S&L apps | yes | no | yes | no | no |
| Socrates Jones | no | yes (Western) | yes | no | loosely |
| **Moksha Patam** | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |

Nobody is treating Indian philosophy as a live argument you can play. The existing
Indian-board products use the board as a mirror for the player's own life, which is a
good product and a different one. The philosophy-teaching games are Western and mostly
a decade old. And citing the Chāndogya by chapter and verse on the card, in both
languages, is apparently unique.

## 6. Three things worth taking

**The Nyāya debate square, built on Socrates Jones' mechanic.** Indian logic supplies
the parts ready-made: the *pañcāvayava* five-member inference, *vyāpti* and the
*upādhi* that invalidates it, and *hetvābhāsa*, a catalogue of named fallacy types that
is effectively a list of failure moves. The native text structure is already a debate,
since every treatise states the *pūrvapakṣa* before its own *siddhānta*. A square that
makes you state the opponent's view at full strength before choosing your own would be
authentic, mechanically interesting, and as far as this scan found, has never been
built for Indian logic. If one idea from this file survives, make it this one.

**Entry on a six.** Both Leela and the gyanchaupar board gate entry on a specific roll,
and the current game already has a "not yet born" state. Keep it and give it the
doctrine: you do not choose to be born, and you wait for it.

**Infinite Play Mode.** gyanchaupar continues after someone reaches the end. For a game
about saṃsāra that is almost too apt, and it fits the parallel-lives mode in Phase 10.

---

## 7. One warning

leela.guide puts an LLM guide behind every square and paywalls after ten replies. That
is the obvious direction for a game like this and it costs a backend, an API key and
per-user spend, which would end the static single-file property that makes this project
cheap to host and easy to hand to an agent. The Codex covers most of what the AI guide
offers, for free, forever, and offline. Treat the conversational layer as a deliberate
non-goal unless it ever earns its cost.
